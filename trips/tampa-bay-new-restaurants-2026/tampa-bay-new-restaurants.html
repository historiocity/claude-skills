Mei
Downtown St. Petersburg
French technique, Japanese ingredients (with a Nordic streak)
9.0
Best special-occasion pick
Pros Tampa Bay Times critic Helen Freund called it "a great — and very welcome — addition" that steps up St. Pete fine dining. Spanish, wood-fired (tapas, paella, Ibérico)

Rio Izakaya
Westshore Marina District, South Tampa
Japanese: binchōtan yakitori, dry-aged sushi
8.7
Best value at a high level
Pros 4.8 on Yelp across 33 reviews. Reviewers call it "a great restaurant on all counts: quality, timing, service, ambiance and originality" and "something Tampa needed." Rare cuts (heart, shoulder, cartilage), the Wagyu Trinity skewer, salmon tartine, chef's nigiri and the rare-sake list all get praise. Warm amber, minimalist woodwork, counter view of the grills.
Cons Izakaya format means shared skewers and small plates, not a coursed white-tablecloth dinner (unless you book the omakase). No major critic review yet. The Westshore Marina District isn't a dinner neighborhood you'd stroll.
Yelp 4.8 (33 reviews)
 Japanese is on your list, and this is the purist end of it: named technique (binchōtan), dry-aged fish, daily-milled rice. It's the only true yakitori omakase in the area, and the most consistently glowing user reviews of any opening this year. May 2026

Tommy's Chophouse
Ybor City, Tampa
Modern steakhouse (American Wagyu, butcher cuts, raw bar)
8.0
Best steak pick, independent
Pros 4.7 on Yelp (14 reviews) and 5.0 on Tripadvisor. Diners mention steaks and fish "cooked perfectly" and a "very nice interior" with friendly staff. Snake River Farms Wagyu cooked on a 750° plancha and a 1,000° broiler. Lesser-known cuts (Denver, hanger, picanha). Seafood towers built on Versaggi shrimp and Calusa oysters. Strong Champagne list.
Cons Pitched as an "approachable," "vibrant, social" steakhouse, so it may run livelier than you'd like. The menu leans toward hanger and picanha over top-tier filet and ribeye. 7th Avenue nightlife is right outside. The group's earlier Ybor spot, Barterhouse, closed.

What didn't make it

Considered and cut

  • The Landon South Howard, Tampa Opened late March 2026, just outside your 6-month window. The Hell's Kitchen chef runs a street-food-leaning menu (birria dumplings, luxe wings), and reviewers say the room is very loud. A near-miss.
  • V Modern Italian Rome Collective, Tampa Third US outpost of an international restaurant group (after Nashville and Charleston), which breaks your no-chains rule. It opened in September and has no meaningful reviews yet.
  • O-Ku Bayshore Gardens, Tampa Polished Japanese, but it's a multi-city chain born in Charleston.
  • Forbici Modern Italian Sundial, St. Pete Opened June 24. It's the group's second Forbici, reviewers repeatedly say live music makes it "very loud," and the menu sprawls (burgers, grain bowls, wings).
  • The Sapphire Westshore, Tampa A 1950s supper club with VIP suites, live music and a late-night menu. Too scene-y for your taste.
  • Origen Dunedin Opened in June. It's a Tulum-inspired Mexican and steak spot with a rooftop. The vibe-forward, tequila-led concept doesn't fit, though it's the only notable north-Pinellas opening.
  • Bosc Modern Bar + Kitchen Hyde Park, Tampa Opened in February, outside the window. Sushi, steaks and pizza until 2am make it a generalist late-night spot.
  • Sundrop at The Luce St. Pete Beach Opened in June inside a Marriott Tribute hotel. The coastal menu is competent but generic resort dining.
  • Bread & Vine Westchase Town Center A French café-bistro open 9am–8pm with burgers and happy hour. Not a dinner destination.
  • 31 South St. Petersburg Distillery Opened May 1. It's a 45-seat speakeasy serving bites from Chef Johnathan Rodriguez, so it's a cocktail stop rather than a dinner. Good for a drink before or after.
  • Kojo (Central Park food hall) Downtown St. Pete Pan-Asian, a cuisine you skip, and it opened in February.

Not open yet, worth watching

  • Moon by Malio's Pass-a-Grille Wild-caught-seafood fine dining from the Malio's owners. It was slated for September, but I couldn't confirm it has opened, and there are no reviews yet.
  • White Wolf + Carnivore Club Westshore Blvd, Tampa Tastes Pretty Good (Rocca): a rustic Italian restaurant with a 28–34-seat wood-fired meat counter upstairs. Both are still "coming soon." This is the opening I'd watch most closely.
  • Kingfish Rome Collective, Tampa Tastes Pretty Good's minimalist sushi bar, now pushed to Q1 2027.
  • Tokyo Swim Club Casa Gomez rooftop, Ybor An izakaya and cocktail rooftop from the Kōsen team, due fall 2026.
  • Bararam St. Pete Beach A small Spanish tapas and sherry bar from the owners of Sola Bistro, due this fall.
  • Toro Odessa (Gunn Hwy) The Koya/Noble Rice team's casual sushi and ramen spot, due around October. It's the closest quality opening to Trinity and Westchase, but casual.
  • Peddler + Potter Oyster House Hyde Park (former Haven) Bern's Steak House Company's seafood hearth, due fall 2027.
Gaps, method and sources

North Pinellas, Pasco, Westchase, Oldsmar and Trinity: no high-end dinner opening in the last six months met the bar. Openings there were casual or chains (Nueva Cantina, Bread & Vine, Saucy by KFC, Bonchon). The closest worthwhile drives are Lumbre and Rio Izakaya, both in South Tampa off the Courtney Campbell or Veterans. Toro in Odessa (Koya team) is the one to watch nearby.

Scene-setting: 2026 has been a strong year for Tampa Bay openings, but most of the energy went into food halls, fast-casual and chain expansion. The serious openings mostly come from groups with Michelin history (Tastes Pretty Good, the Tung brothers' Omei group). Several of the most promising projects (White Wolf, Carnivore Club, Kingfish, Tokyo Swim Club) haven't opened yet.

Method: I swept the monthly openings roundups (Tampa Magazine, Creative Loafing, St Pete Rising, That's So Tampa, Tampa Bay Times, Axios), then checked each candidate's reviews on Yelp, OpenTable, Tripadvisor and Resy, plus editorial reviews. I filtered against the dining rules in your trip-planner preferences (no chains, no scene-y rooms, welcome cuisines) and dropped the $300 ceiling as you asked. Websites were checked live on Oct 3, 2026. Instagram and Facebook pages couldn't be read directly, so I relied on Yelp and OpenTable for recent guest sentiment.

Verify before booking: Marcel St. Pete is three weeks old, so check that hours and menu have settled. For Tommy's Chophouse and Marcel, the Yelp links run a search because neither has a stable Yelp page yet.
